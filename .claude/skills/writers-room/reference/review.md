# Review rubric (Braintrust)

Used by the braintrust subagent after every stage. The reviewer reads this file, anti-slop.md, conflicts.md, and the lenses in writers-and-directors.md that match the project.

## How the Braintrust works (from Pixar, adapted)
- The reviewer has no authority. Notes diagnose; Leonard decides. [Catmull: the Braintrust "had no authority" and "could not tell the director what to do"]
- Candor is the whole point. Say what is not working, where, and what it does to an audience. No softening, no compliments as padding. [Catmull: "We need a lot of candor"]
- Every draft starts broken; the review assumes problems exist and looks for them. [Catmull: "all of our films start out as disasters"]
- Notes are about the work, never the writer.
- A note may suggest a direction to explore; it never rewrites the work and never substitutes a new idea for Leonard's.
- Fresh eyes: the reviewer reads only the files it is given, as an audience would.

## Note format
```
N[n] [BLOCKING|MAJOR|MINOR] <file>:<scene/beat/line>
Problem: <what is not working, in one or two sentences>
Evidence: <quote or exact location>
Effect: <what this does to the audience: confusion, boredom, disbelief, predicted ending, lost empathy...>
Principle: <craft.md # / McKee ch. / anti-slop # / lens>
Direction (optional): <a question or direction to explore, not a rewrite>
```
After the notes: a short list "Load-bearing, keep" naming elements that work and must survive revision (only if removing them would be a real risk). Then: "Open questions for Leonard" if a note depends on his intent.

Severity
- BLOCKING: the story can't work until this is fixed (no climax, no desire, stakes absent, scenes that don't turn, a broken non-negotiable, an unlabeled replacement of Leonard's idea).
- MAJOR: weakens the film noticeably (weak antagonism, predictable turns, on-the-nose dialogue in key scenes, sagging act).
- MINOR: line-level and polish.

## Checks for every stage
1. Fidelity: every element traces to Leonard's input or is labeled [PROPOSED]. Compare [MINE] items word for word with his original: facts turned into beliefs, conjunctions changed, causes narrowed, slots moved or emptied all count as unlabeled replacement (BLOCKING). Non-negotiables intact. Adaptations: nothing added beyond conflicts.md A.14.
2. Universality: can someone with no knowledge of the place, period or subculture follow and feel it? Is the specific detail present (not generic)?
3. Anti-slop pass: run anti-slop.md section E.
4. Em dash: none anywhere.

## Stage checks

### Restatement and story bible (steps 2 and 4)
- Is the protagonist's conscious desire concrete? Is there an unconscious desire that contradicts it?
- Are stakes total for this character? Answer McKee's test: if he fails, does life go back to normal?
- Are the forces of antagonism mapped on three levels and down to the Negation of the Negation?
- Does a climax exist? Is the controlling idea one sentence, value plus cause, derived from the climax?
- Formula A to D complete? Central question in three layers?
- Story triangle position stated and consistent with the vibe?
- Is the assessment honest (names what's generic) and free of praise padding?

### Beat sheet (step 5)
- Inciting incident on screen, early enough, ripe?
- Every progressive complication a point of no return, escalating in kind?
- Three or more major reversals for a feature; last two act climaxes opposite in charge?
- Crisis is a true dilemma; climax answers the major dramatic question irreversibly; resolution brief.
- Any structure break declared with all five lines (structure.md section 4)?
- Runtime math respected (structure.md section 5)?

### Scene list (step 5)
- Every scene: value, opening charge, closing charge differ. List every scene that doesn't turn.
- Turning on action vs revelation balanced; revelations saved for act climaxes.
- Setups and payoffs tracked: anything planted and never used; anything paid off but never planted.
- Repetition: consecutive scenes with the same emotional charge (Law of Diminishing Returns).
- Scene length variety; tempo rises toward climaxes.

### Treatment (step 6)
- Present tense, moment by moment, with subtext stated (what characters think and feel under what they do). No dialogue except essential lines.
- Exposition dramatized as ammunition; nothing explained that can be assumed.
- Each character's choices credible from inside his point of view; antagonists not villainous by label.
- Images systems and telling details present.

### Screenplay (step 6)
- Fountain format valid; present tense; no "we see"; no camera directions beyond necessary inserts and POV.
- Dialogue: subtext, distinct voices, short speeches, no announced feelings, no aphorism endings, interruptions with double hyphen.
- Each scene playable without its dialogue? Mark scenes that are talking heads.
- Page count vs runtime target.
- Opening pages: does the film announce its world and rhythm?
- Last pages: is anything explained that the climax already showed? Is something lost?

## Lens selection
Pick 2 to 4 lenses from writers-and-directors.md:
- Always: Catmull (review culture) is the frame, not a lens.
- Vibe-matched: e.g. suspense or adventure → Spielberg early, Cameron, Stine; historical or realist → Spielberg historical, Kubrick, Ridley Scott; inner or formally unusual → Kaufman, Garland; warmth or ensemble → Howard, Zemeckis; raw, lived material → Bukowski, King; momentum and style → Boyle, Wilbur Smith.
- State which lenses were used at the top of the review.
