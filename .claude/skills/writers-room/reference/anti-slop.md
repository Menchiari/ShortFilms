# Anti-slop: AI writing tells and their fixes

Load before writing any prose, treatment or screenplay page, and at every Braintrust review. Every tell: how to detect it, how to fix it.

Evidence base
- [study] Chakrabarty et al., "Can AI writing be salvaged?" (arXiv 2409.14509): 18 professional writers edited 1,057 paragraphs from GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet and Llama 3.1 70B. Seven categories of problems, similar across all three models. Most frequent: awkward word choice and phrasing (28% of edits), poor sentence structure (20%), unnecessary exposition (18%), cliché (17%). Recurring model phrases: "sense of", "mix of", "weight of".
- [McKee] principles that the tell violates.
- [Leonard] tells named in his brief.
- [observed] patterns seen in model output, not from a study. Treat as likely, not proven.

## A. Character and dialogue

1. Characters announce their feelings. [Leonard; McKee ch. 11, 17]
   Detect: "I feel...", "I'm so angry", "I'm scared that...", a character naming his own emotion or motive.
   Fix: cut the line; give the feeling a behavior or a choice with a cost. If a line must stay, make it a deflection or a lie.

2. Everyone is equally articulate. [Leonard; McKee ch. 8, 18]
   Detect: all characters speak in complete, well-formed sentences with similar vocabulary and rhythm.
   Fix: assign each character a speech profile in the bible (education, region, register, what they avoid naming); cut words from the inarticulate; let someone fail to answer.

3. Characters explain themselves or each other. [McKee ch. 17; template's "Do you know what your problem is?"]
   Detect: diagnosis speeches, backstory confessions to near-strangers ("California scenes"), a friend summarizing the hero's flaw.
   Fix: delete; test the trait through a choice under pressure instead.

4. Therapy vocabulary in characters who wouldn't use it. [observed]
   Detect: "boundaries", "process", "trauma", "I need you to hear me", "space", "valid", in period or regional characters.
   Fix: use the character's own vocabulary and era; often silence or a joke.

5. Conflict resolved by a good conversation. [McKee ch. 9; observed]
   Detect: two characters talk it out and agree; apologies accepted within the scene.
   Fix: people rarely change their minds in one talk. Make the talk fail, or make it succeed at a price that creates the next problem.

6. Everyone is reasonable and kind. [observed]
   Detect: no one is petty, unfair, boring, rude, or wrong for long; antagonists are misunderstood.
   Fix: give characters real self-interest; let them be wrong and stay wrong; build the negative side to its limit (McKee ch. 14).

7. Villain explains his plan or philosophy. [McKee ch. 17; observed]
   Detect: monologue of motive, especially at the climax.
   Fix: the villain acts to get away with it; motive emerges from his choices.

8. Names said constantly in dialogue. [observed]
   Detect: characters addressing each other by name in most lines.
   Fix: keep names only where a real person would use them (calling, anger, intimacy).

## B. Scene construction

9. Scenes end on an aphorism or a wise line. [Leonard]
   Detect: final line is a general truth, a quotable sentence, or a summary of the scene.
   Fix: end on an action, a reaction, or a question left open; cut the last line and see if the scene is better.

10. "It's not X, it's Y" / "This isn't about X. It's about Y." [Leonard; observed]
    Detect: negation followed by correction, in dialogue or description.
    Fix: say Y directly, or better, show Y; keep X out.

11. Lists of three. [Leonard; observed]
    Detect: triads of adjectives, nouns or clauses ("tired, broke and alone"; "the smell, the noise, the heat").
    Fix: choose the single most specific item; if two are needed, use two.

12. Theme explained out loud at the end. [Leonard; McKee ch. 6, 13; conflicts.md A.5]
    Detect: a character states the lesson, the moral, or what it all meant.
    Fix: delete; make the climax action carry it without words.

13. Tidy reconciliation. [Leonard; McKee ch. 6 on irony]
    Detect: every relationship mended, every subplot closed, everyone forgiven, hugs in the last scene.
    Fix: decide what is lost; leave one relationship broken or changed; let the gain cost something visible.

14. Every character gets an arc. [observed]
    Detect: supporting characters each learn a lesson.
    Fix: most supporting roles bring out a side of the protagonist and stay who they are (McKee ch. 17).

15. Turning points signposted. [McKee ch. 10; observed]
    Detect: "Little did he know", "Everything was about to change", ominous music cues in action lines, dialogue that warns of the twist.
    Fix: plant the setup so it reads innocently; let the turn arrive without announcement.

16. No real dilemma. [McKee ch. 10]
    Detect: the protagonist chooses between good and bad; the right answer is obvious.
    Fix: make both options good or both bad, with equal weight.

## C. Prose and description (treatment and action lines)

17. Abstract emotional nouns. [study]
    Detect: "a sense of", "a mix of", "the weight of", "a wave of", "something shifted".
    Fix: name the physical thing that causes or shows it.

18. Unnecessary or redundant exposition. [study; McKee ch. 15]
    Detect: telling what the scene already showed; restating a reaction; explaining a joke or a symbol.
    Fix: delete the restatement.

19. Purple prose. [study; McKee ch. 18]
    Detect: stacked adjectives and adverbs, metaphors that can't be photographed, "as if" in action lines.
    Fix: precise noun plus precise verb; only what the camera can see or the mic can hear.

20. Lack of specificity. [study; McKee ch. 3]
    Detect: "a bustling city", "an old café", "a small town", "a worn jacket", generic weather, generic jobs.
    Fix: the one detail only this place, period and person would have. If it isn't known, it's a research item, not a guess.

21. Awkward hedging and passive voice. [study]
    Detect: "seems to", "appears to", "begins to", "starts to", "is being".
    Fix: the action itself, active, present tense.

22. Tense drift. [study]
    Detect: past tense in action lines or treatment.
    Fix: present tense throughout screenplay and treatment.

23. Stock body cues. [observed]
    Detect: breath held without knowing, jaw clenching, eyes glistening, a smile tugging, heart pounding, knuckles white.
    Fix: a behavior specific to this character (what he does with his hands when he lies), or cut.

24. Weather and light mirror the mood. [observed; McKee ch. 12 on mood vs emotion]
    Detect: rain when sad, sun when happy, the light "changes" at the turn.
    Fix: weather is set by place and date; counterpoint is usually stronger.

25. Pointed symbols. [McKee ch. 18]
    Detect: an object described as meaningful, or returning with commentary.
    Fix: let image systems repeat without comment; never label.

26. Em dashes. [Leonard]
    Detect: the em dash character (Unicode U+2014) anywhere; also the en dash (U+2013) used as punctuation.
    Fix: comma, period, colon, parentheses, or double hyphen for dialogue interruptions in Fountain.

27. "We see", "we hear", camera directions. [McKee ch. 18]
    Fix: describe what is on screen; imply shots through paragraphing.

## D. Story-level tells

28. Generic names. [Leonard; observed]
    Detect: names that recur in model output across stories (for example Elara, Kai, Marcus, Mira, Sarah Chen) or names that don't fit place, class, region and birth year.
    Fix: choose names from the character's real world: birth year, region, family tradition, class. For Italian characters, consider regional distribution and saints' names in families. Record the reason in the bible.

29. The default premise. [observed]
    Detect: the "obvious" version of the idea, the one that appears first.
    Fix: McKee's list method: invent 5 to 15 versions of the key scene or premise, choose the truest one that hasn't been seen.

30. Borrowed scenes. [McKee ch. 3]
    Detect: a scene recognizable from a famous film.
    Fix: keep the mechanism, rebuild the content from this story's world.

31. Balanced, safe meaning. [McKee ch. 6; observed]
    Detect: ending that offends no one; both sides equally right; message nobody could disagree with.
    Fix: take a position the story earns; let the counter-idea fight at full strength first.

32. Marketing voice. [Leonard]
    Detect: taglines, loglines written like posters, adjectives like "gripping", "heartfelt", "unforgettable".
    Fix: describe what happens. No taglines anywhere.

33. Replacing the writer's idea with a better-sounding one. [Leonard]
    Detect: an element not traceable to the user's input and not marked [PROPOSED].
    Fix: label it, or remove it.

34. Reinterpreting the writer's facts while tagging them as his. [found while testing this system]
    Detect: a fact he stated becomes a belief of the character ("she is a coward" becomes "she thinks she's a coward"); "and" becomes "or"; his cause becomes a different cause; a slot he filled is marked open.
    Fix: restore his version as [MINE]; put the reading beside it as [PROPOSED]; if ambiguous, ask.

35. Slogans and praise in analysis. [found while testing this system]
    Detect: short quotable verdicts ("The stakes speak for themselves.", "This writes itself.", "Few ideas have..."), compliments, overclaims ("each step can't be undone" when one can).
    Fix: one plain claim, the audience effect, the evidence. If it can't be checked, cut it.

36. Invented specifics in assessments. [found while testing this system]
    Detect: concrete details the writer never gave (a car, a letter, a daily habit) used as if established.
    Fix: remove, or tag [EXAMPLE] / [PROPOSED].

37. Default endings offered as options. [found while testing this system]
    Detect: candidate endings that are the first ones the premise suggests (crime doesn't pay; the underdog who turns out to be gifted).
    Fix: list method on the climax; label defaults [DEFAULT]; include one candidate that gives the opposing side its full case.

38. Antithetical flourish. [found while testing this system]
    Detect: a clause that closes on a balanced twist or a verdict: "the victory is real and so is its price", "winning the war also ends the only life he knew", "That is what makes it a dilemma".
    Fix: state the plain fact or the plain consequence; cut the twist. In analysis, claims are tested, not crowned.

39. A summary that answers an open question. [found while testing this system]
    Detect: a logline or synopsis that settles something still listed as a question.
    Fix: while the question is open, use the writer's exact words for that part.

## E. Self-check before handing anything over
1. Search the draft for: the em dash (U+2014; e.g. `grep -nP '\x{2014}' file`), "sense of", "mix of", "weight of", "seems to", "begins to", "starts to", "It's not", "isn't about", "we see", "we hear", "suddenly", "little did".
2. Read every scene's last line: is it an aphorism? Cut it.
3. Count triads in description; break them.
4. For each character, read only his lines in sequence: could they belong to another character?
5. Check every proposed element is labeled [PROPOSED].
6. Check the ending: what is lost?
