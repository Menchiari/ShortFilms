## Keeping the vision

1. **The shots you see clearly** get a start frame from your pipeline: the video starts exactly on
   your image, Seedance only animates it.
2. **The crossroads** (43 shots, half the film) is built once in 3D: buildings, the street light,
   the car, the landing area, the sun. Twelve camera setups, one grey render each: you paint over
   them, so geography and light match in every angle.
3. **Moves words cannot hold** (car over the camera, the landing, the dolly zoom): a grey render of
   the move becomes the video reference.
4. **Frame chaining:** a frame of an approved take seeds the next shot of the same setup.

## Keeping the color

1. **At the source:** every reference and start frame is made with the two palette images as style
   reference (`look/`). Afternoon for S02 to S05, warming through S06 and S07, sunset for S08 to
   S10 and the end of S01.
2. **In every prompt:** the same look line, plus the palette image of that scene as a reference
   for color and light only.
3. **In the edit:** one base grade built from the palette card on the whole timeline, then Shot
   Match in Resolve for anything that drifts.

## Credits

## Solutions that carry over

- **Filters** (gun at the temple, hanging, firebombs, the stain): tested Monday morning at 480p.
  Every risky shot has a written fallback: cut on the action, the hit out of frame, sound carries it.
- **Same old man in 60 shots, four states:** B, C, D are edits of the same A image.
- **Text on screen** (signs, gas board, bond, Agreement, phone, scope, TV): never generated
  readable. Start and end frames carry the exact text, or I composite it.
- **Shots under 4 s** (64 of 105): runs of short shots in one place become one multi-shot
  generation; the rest at 4 s, trimmed.
- **Voices:** generated with the takes for lip sync, then each character unified with one voice.
- **Music:** never in the takes. Original cue on Wednesday 25 if you want one.

## What can break it

1. **References after Monday:** each late day moves the end by about a day. If you are still
   sick, I generate the references with the image model you name (credits, your approval).
2. **A filter refuses a core beat:** the fallback changes the film; you decide the same day.
3. **Your hours:** this needs about 4 to 5 hours a day from you on Monday and Tuesday, less after.
   If that is too much, I prepare a list of shots that can go without hurting the story.
