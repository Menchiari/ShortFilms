N1 [MAJOR] screenplay.fountain: S09 SH120 to SH130 (the final line)
Problem: At first hearing the line gets the audience to "of course, the crisis was their lever" and stops there. His "holy fuck" needs a second step: they set the price, so they can raise it again, and the dead died over a price that drops on Monday. Nothing after the line prompts that step, and four things weaken the first one.
Evidence:
1. What the words say. Taken literally, the deal ends the crisis and "You fixed it." It is past tense, about a finished job. His ending is "They hop on the helicopter and move on, ready to fuck the world up."
2. Shape. "Now that it's signed, fuel comes down on Monday. / (a beat) / You fixed it." is setup, pause, verdict. The tag says the irony out loud, and the first thing it sounds like is "good job". If his "the pacing is right" was about the rhythm of the draft 2 line, that was one sentence with the pause inside it. This is a sentence, a beat and a second sentence.
3. The setup. The price board is 3 s at 0:05, one of eight riot images. "fuel up again overnight" is one of three anchor voices talking over each other. Nothing on screen ties his empty tank to the crisis, so the shot note's "pays off ... the empty tank" is an echo, not a payoff. The thing the film sets up most is his face: the signs, the TV portrait, the recognition, the hanging. His own Q2 answer is about exactly that ("this one is the one that has been blamed, for money... using reputation as a weapon"), and the line gives it only the three-word tag.
4. Hearing and time. She speaks next to a helicopter with the rotors turning ("low under the rotors" in SH070, and it lifts off right after). "Monday" is the word that has to be heard, and her accent isn't specified. SH120 is 7 s but holds about 8 to 9 s of action: closing the folder, two looks, the line, the beat and the tag (my estimate). SH130 is 3 s and holds his reaction, her turn, her walk and his boarding. The audience thinks about the line while he reacts, and that gets under a second.
Effect: most viewers come away with a smart "of course", not the nausea and rage he asked for. A viewer who takes it at face value can read him as the man who was bringing the fix, and the crowd as wrong to go after him. The tag plays as the villain's exit quip, the genre default the draft 2 review warned about.
Principle: Garland lens (meaning by inference); dialogue.md 2 (the subtext sentence is spoken); anti-slop 9, 38; structure.md 5.
Compared with alternatives (types of line taken from his own words; these are not new lines):
- The price (this draft): the thinnest setup. Good news on the surface, control underneath.
- His face and the blame (his Q2 and Q10 answers): the most set-up element in the film. It answers "why do they still want him after his reputation went so much to shit" directly.
- Looking forward (his "let's get back to work", "a lot of work left to be done"): points where his ending points and needs no setup. It is the closest to the genre default.
Direction: Play SH120 with only the first sentence and hold on his reaction. Is it stronger or weaker? Which half should carry the "of course": the price or his face?

N2 [MINOR] screenplay.fountain: S06 SH050 to SH070 (the gun moment, assumption 2)
Problem: The crowd's distance doesn't add up in real time. The shot that is supposed to show time slowing can't see the crowd.
Evidence:
- "The rest break into a sprint, twenty meters away" comes early in a 7 s shot, and SH060 (3 s) is also real time: about 8 s in all. A sprinting crowd covers 20 m in about 3 s.
- SH070 is a CLOSE on him, but the action is the crowd "in front of him" slowing and him looking "at the faces". With the camera facing him, the crowd is behind it. The size column says "slow push in" and the movement column says "Locked-off".
- "His jaw sets." comes at the beat he asked to be "more intense" (anti-slop 23).
Effect: the generation gets a close-up of a man and no visible slowdown, and in the edit the crowd should already have reached him.
Direction: How far away is the crowd at "One."? During the sound drain, what does the camera see: him, them, or both?

N3 [MINOR] screenplay.fountain: S07 SH070, S09 SH040 (his limit on explicit images)
Problem: In SH070 the camera is "Wide and from behind the man" for a shot to the face. The bullet exits the back of the head, so a camera behind him looks straight at the exit. That is the splattered head he ruled out ("unnecessay satisfaction"). In SH040 he spits on "the nearest body" in a MEDIUM with no framing note, while every other shot near the dead has one.
Effect: both shots can produce, when generated, the image he excluded.
Direction: Which side of the man does the camera see? How is the body framed?

N4 [MINOR] prop-agreement.txt (the page)
Problem: The riots are in "more than forty countries", but the page is an agreement with "THE GOVERNMENT OF THE TERRITORY". Heard at first, "fuel comes down" sounds global, which is the bigger idea. A viewer who pauses finds it covers one territory. Article 12.3 spells the line out ("shall credit the reduction to the Grantor's signatory"), which is fine as a reward for pausing. ASSUMPTIONS
1. Bobblehead not gold, hand on heart: holds. The Trump markers are gone, and a hand on the heart is a generic American pose. Two conditions: the likeness has to read in 2 s (S02 SH080), and the last nod after "You fixed it" now carries his rebuilt public image, so the figure has to be clearly him.
2. Gun moment in his head: holds. The sound drain plus a slowing crowd reads as subjective. Fix the distance and the framing (N2). The device itself (sound drops and the world slows before a death) is the most familiar version of this beat.
3. Helicopter high, far and unheard: holds for sound, since it arrives during the noise of the chase, after the town's silence. The cost: the farther away it holds, the harder it is to believe it hits a rope from a hover. This is still an open research item in the bible.
