## Logline

## Story triangle position
- Ending: closed (he leaves alive, the crowd is broken).
- Conflict: external (crowd, town, fuel), with one inner reveal (the spit).
- Protagonist: single, active until the gun is empty, then passive; the rescue is done to him. [PROPOSED reading]

- Causality: the rescue is causal only if planted (the call, the reporter's warning). Without those two plants it reads as coincidence.

## Honest assessment
### Strong
- The trap: walls on both sides, one road through, the fuel gauge falling [MINE]. The audience understands the geography and the clock in two images, which a short needs.
- The recognition: one man ("Is it really you?"), a friend texting, faces in the windows, voices from the buildings [MINE]. The danger grows by sound before anyone runs, and it matches how crowds form through phones.
- The money flying [MINE]: the crowd's rage stops for cash. The image shows the outrage being bought for a moment, without a word, and it can pay off in the ending.
- The cut call, "the add--" [MINE]: if the rescuers arrive anyway, the audience realizes they were tracked from the start. This plants the "tech that exists but nobody can see" with no explanation.
- The mob that thins to "the tough ones" who walk slowly [MINE]: the crowd is not innocent, so the film does not become a sermon.
- Cutting the brains [MINE decision]: the expected payoff is replaced by a reversal, which is what an ending needs to be unexpected.
- "That's enough Roger" and the candy taken away [MINE]: one beat gives Roger a character and shows killing as routine for these people.
- The spit [MINE]: one action shows who he is, with no speech.

### Generic
- "Let's get back to work" / "a lot of work left to be done" [MINE]: the corrupt-man-walks-away ending is familiar; it confirms what the audience already believes, so there is no "oh shit. Of course." He named this himself.
- The riot montage [MINE]: a burning police truck, smoke at sunset and a news anchor are the default way films show collapse. In 2-3 minutes it uses 30 to 60 seconds to say what the town scene shows anyway. The signs with devil marks, red crossed eyes and him "photoshopped hung" [MINE] are specific and come from life; they are the part to keep.
- The hateable man as a look (botox, fat, tech-styled) [MINE options]: any of these is a costume. What makes him hateable on screen is behavior; the spit is the one he has.
- The business lady [MINE]: a type until she wants something of her own (Q4).
- "Sexy reporter" [MINE]: a type; fine as texture if she returns (candidate 2 uses her twice).
- Killing "just for fun" from the helicopter [MINE] next to "nothing too explicit" [MINE] and the cut brains: a sniper massacre shown in detail is more explicit than the brains he removed. Both stay his; the way to hold both is coverage (distance, sound, the crowd's reaction), decided later.

### Missing
- Length: Part 1 alone is about 4 minutes on the page; riots, desert road, twist and ending add 2 to 4 more. At 2-3 minutes most of Part 1 and most of the riot intro go. Q1.
- The reason they still want him: the ending has no content yet. Q5.
- What he did: "All of this because of this one person" [MINE] needs one concrete, readable cause, or the riots stay generic. Q6.
- What the lady wants for herself. Q4.
- A planted reason for the rescue: without the cut call and the reporter's warning, the helicopter reads as a deus ex machina.
